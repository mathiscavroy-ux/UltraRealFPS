# UltraRealFPS — Art Direction

## Visual target
Grounded tactical industrial realism. The environment should feel like a private training / logistics / maintenance compound rather than a generic arena.

## Palette
- Concrete: cool neutral grey, slightly dirty.
- Asphalt: very dark charcoal, not pure black.
- Structural steel: dark graphite.
- Painted metal: desaturated navy / industrial blue.
- Safety color: restrained amber / yellow.
- Utility accent: faded orange.
- Wood: muted brown, low saturation.
- Medical/ammo interactables keep stronger gameplay colors.

## Surface language
### Concrete
Rough, matte, mild tonal breakup, edge wear, dark water/dirt accumulation near the base.

### Painted steel
Semi-matte, directional wear, chipped edges only on exposed zones, not uniform rust everywhere.

### Bare steel
Higher metallic response, medium roughness, subtle grime.

### Asphalt
Dark, high roughness, broad low-frequency variation, painted lines and oil/dust patches.

### Wood
Visible grain direction, rough, darker joints and contact wear.

## Lighting target
- Clear daytime readability.
- Stronger directional shadow shape than the current flat blockout.
- Exterior remains neutral/cool.
- Utility interiors and maintenance accents may be warmer.
- No crushed black facades.
- No overexposed white ground.

## Landmark language
- Central OPS structure: strongest central framing element.
- East warehouse: wide loading-bay rhythm + blue metal accents.
- West CQB block: heavier concrete mass + orange/safety accents.
- North tower: vertical skyline landmark.
- South maintenance lane: pipe/tank silhouette.

## Prop clustering
Props are grouped by function:
- Logistics: pallet + crate + rack + container.
- Maintenance: cabinet + pipes + HVAC + barriers.
- Safety: bollards + striped marking + concrete barrier.
- Storage: shelves + boxes + wrapped pallets.

## Things to avoid
- Pure black walls.
- Random single crates in empty space.
- Perfectly repeated spacing.
- Full-scene yellow/orange grading.
- Excessive fog masking poor geometry.
- Tiny details used as a substitute for architecture.
